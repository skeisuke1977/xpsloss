# xpsloss

xpsloss is a Python library and command-line tool for researchers reproducing published models of electron energy loss in X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS). Version 0.2.0 covers oscillator energy-loss functions (ELFs), bulk differential inverse inelastic mean free paths in an infinite medium, causal dielectric response including hard gaps, fixed-depth XPS cross sections for gapless materials and materials with a hard band gap, and their depth average at normal emission (version 0.1.0 covered gapless fixed depth only). The unreleased development version (0.3.0.dev0) adds the zero-loss and single-loss spectrum of Eq. (19) of Simonsen et al. (1997) and finite loss-window areas at normal emission, with a choice of weight length and multiplier (below). [日本語版](README.ja.md)

For emission at 0° from the surface normal and a positive creation depth, xpsloss implements a fixed-depth effective cross section based on Eqs. (13)–(17) of Simonsen, Yubero and Tougaard (1997), for gapless materials and for materials with a hard band gap (below). The caller chooses the momentum argument of the dielectric function. `k_parallel` uses the approximation made in the closed-form derivation. At normal emission, `k_total` recovers the dispersive bulk limit. The two choices are checked differently. An independent numerical route (I3) covers `k_parallel` for dispersionless and dispersive inputs. `k_total` is checked through the bulk identity of term 13a, the deep limit, and a second implementation of the same closed forms; for dispersive inputs, no independent check covers all finite-depth terms. For dispersionless inputs the two choices coincide. Following the paper, the model assumes that the electron and the core hole are created suddenly at the same point at t = 0, that the hole is static with infinite lifetime, that the electron moves on a rectilinear trajectory at a constant velocity set by the initial kinetic energy (nonrelativistic), that losses are small compared with E0, and a specular-reflection model of the surface. Oblique emission, depth averaging with trajectory-dependent or self-consistent lengths, loss-window areas, and spectra are outside release 0.2.0; multiple scattering and spectra beyond single loss are not implemented in any version. [Equations and units (Japanese)](docs/EQUATIONS.md)

xpsloss is independent of QUEELS-XPS, QUASES and their authors. It is not an official, endorsed, or compatibility-guaranteed version of QUEELS-XPS and uses none of its executables, DLLs, source code, or figures.

## Quick start

From the root of the v0.2.0 source tree, use Python 3.10 or later in a virtual environment. The example material is synthetic.

```sh
python -m pip install .
xpsloss capabilities --json
xpsloss validate examples/synthetic_bulk.json
xpsloss bulk examples/synthetic_bulk.json --out runs/bulk
xpsloss dielectric examples/synthetic_bulk.json --q-inv-A 0.5 --out runs/dielectric
xpsloss xps-fixed-depth examples/synthetic_bulk.json --depth-A 10 --epsilon-momentum k_total --out runs/fixed-depth
xpsloss xps-depth-averaged examples/synthetic_bulk.json --weight-length-A 20 --epsilon-momentum k_total --out runs/depth-averaged
```

Give each calculation a new output directory. The CSV contains values on the loss-energy grid. For fixed depth these include the total, the electron contribution with zero hole charge, and the signed unit-hole contribution including interference, in Å⁻¹ eV⁻¹ per path length in the solid. The accompanying `run.json` records the input path as supplied, its SHA-256, units, model, numerical settings, and runtime versions.

For `xps-fixed-depth`, `run.json` also has an `applicability` block. It records the model assumptions, the largest loss divided by E0, the checked bounds (E0 of 1000–4000 eV, max T/E0 of 0.1) with their basis, and whether the run lies inside them. At both energy bounds, all eight terms were checked against the independent route with `k_parallel` at discrete losses up to T/E0 = 0.1. Depth, material, line widths, dispersion, hole charge and the lower end of the loss grid are not part of this check. A successful run outside either bound reports a warning. Being inside the bounds establishes neither accuracy for arbitrary inputs nor physical validity.

For a material with a hard band gap (M2-2, since 0.2.0), the dielectric response is the step-function ELF with a Kramers–Kronig real part and no extra broadening. Losses within a narrow exclusion width of the gap edge are rejected. Below the gap the model medium is lossless: a loss is computed only when an outward-rounded analytic bound proves that no undamped surface mode (ε = −1) lies in its momentum range, and is otherwise rejected with `FixedDepthGapDomainError`. At such losses the unit-hole `total` vanishes, while `no_hole` and `hole` are nonzero reactive terms, not absorption. Gapped inputs must also satisfy a per-oscillator monotonicity condition. The gapped checks cover Si from the ELF data set at 1000 eV, depths of 2–200 Å and losses of 0.2–99.8 eV; other gapped inputs get a warning. [Design (Japanese)](docs/M2_2_DESIGN.md)

Since 0.2.0 (M3a), `effective_cross_section_depth_averaged` and the `xps-depth-averaged` command average the fixed-depth outputs over depth at normal emission with the single-scattering path weight of Eq. (18) of Simonsen et al. (1997), x exp(−x/L)/L². The weight length L (`weight_length_A`, `--weight-length-A`) is required, supplied by the caller and constant over all paths; `--weight-length-source` records where it came from. The depth integral is closed analytically inside the momentum integrand, so there is no depth grid. The eleven outputs are in Å⁻¹ eV⁻¹ per path length in the solid, including vacuum losses, and `total = no_hole + hole_charge * hole`. As the paper cautions, Eq. (18) borrows a REELS path weighting whose validity for XPS needs examination, and the result describes only the first inelastic scattering, including intrinsic losses; both cautions are in `warnings`. Trajectory-dependent or self-consistent lengths, elastic scattering, non-uniform depth distributions, loss-window areas and spectra are not implemented. Non-convergence raises `DepthAverageConvergenceError` with a `reason`. The pre-registered checks used Al and Si from the ELF data set and synthetic inputs, almost all at 1000 eV, with lengths of 5, 20 and 80 Å and limits from 10⁻⁶ to 10⁶ Å, for both momentum choices. `run.json` lists the checked conditions but does not judge each run against them. [Design (Japanese)](docs/M3A_DESIGN.md) · [Pre-registration (Japanese)](docs/M3A_PREREG.md)

### Development version (0.3.0.dev0): single-loss spectrum and loss-window area

These two commands exist only in the development version, run from the root of the current source tree. `examples/single_loss_spectrum.json` holds a synthetic primary (one delta line at 1000 eV) and the output bin edges.

```sh
xpsloss xps-loss-window-area examples/synthetic_bulk.json --loss-window-eV 1 50 --weight-length-A 20 --epsilon-momentum k_total --out runs/loss-window-area
xpsloss xps-single-loss examples/synthetic_bulk.json --spectrum examples/single_loss_spectrum.json --loss-window-eV 1 50 --weight-length-A 20 --epsilon-momentum k_total --out runs/single-loss
```

`loss_window_area`, `single_loss_spectrum` and the two commands (M4a) use Eq. (19) of Simonsen et al. (1997) at normal emission with proportionality constant 1 and an externally supplied constant length L. They return the zero-loss term J0 = F and the single-loss satellite J1 = L∫F(E+T)K_sc(E0,T;L)dT. The kernel is the depth-averaged output of M3a, fixed at the configured `electron_energy_eV` (E0) for every primary energy. By default L is used both as the weight length of Eq. (18) and as the multiplier of Eq. (19); the two can be chosen separately (M4a-2, below).

- **The loss window is required.** `--loss-window-eV a b` (`loss_window_eV`, 0 < a < b < E0) has no default. The paper notes that the integral in Eq. (19) diverges and integrates from a cutoff of 0.05 eV (p. 1618), so results can depend on the lower limit. Losses outside the window are neither computed nor included in the balance. The sensitivity to the lower limit (`cutoff_sensitivity`) is returned alongside.
- **Areas.** `window_area.csv` lists, for the eleven columns (eight terms, `no_hole`, `hole`, `total`), the window area in Å⁻¹, its error estimate and budget, and the dimensionless cutoff sensitivity. `run.json` adds `single_loss_weight` (L times the `total` area; dimensionless, not a probability, and it can exceed one) and the inverse area with its interval in Å, which is not called a mean free path.
- **Primary format.** The `--spectrum` JSON has `schema` (`xpsloss-single-loss-spectrum-v0`), `axis` (`kinetic_energy_eV`), `primary` and `output_edges_eV`. The primary is either delta lines (`kind: "delta"`, `energies_eV`, `intensities`) or bins (`kind: "binned"`, `edges_eV`, bin-integrated `intensities`), not both. Intensities are non-negative and positions are non-negative kinetic energies. Output edges are a list or `{"min", "max", "step"}`. The primary is assumed to exclude intrinsic loss satellites.
- **Bin integrals and units.** `single_loss_spectrum.csv` gives, for each output bin (closed on the left and open on the right, except that the last bin includes its right edge) and for the two outside regions (`low`, `high`), the bin integrals of the zero-loss term, the three satellite components (`total`, `no_hole`, `hole`) and their sum `spectrum`, with error estimates, in the intensity unit of the primary. Negative values are not clipped. Densities (intensity unit per eV) are the `*_per_eV` fields of `run.json`. The `loss_grid_eV` of the configuration file is validated but not used.
- **Meaning of success.** Success means that the error estimates of the areas and of every bin are within their budgets; it is not a proven error bound. `diagnostics.resolution_basis` records how strongly the loss-axis resolution is justified. Non-convergence raises `SingleLossConvergenceError` and invalid input `SingleLossInputError`, both with a `reason`; the CLI exits with code 2 and creates no output directory.
- **Weight length and multiplier (M4a-2).** The weight length L_w of Eq. (18) and the multiplier m of Eq. (19) can be chosen separately, so that J1 = m∫F(E+T)K_sc(E0,T;L_w)dT and the single-loss weight is m times the `total` area. The weight length is either the external value (`--weight-length-A`) or `--weight-length-inverse-13a-window-area`, L_w = 1/(A_13a + c), where A_13a is the window area of the 13a term (first computed with a seed length, `--weight-length-seed-A`, 20 Å by default) and c ≥ 0 is an extra inverse length that the caller may supply (`--extra-inverse-length-per-A`); this length depends on the window and is not a mean free path. The multiplier is L_w (default), an external value (`--multiplier-A`) or the inverse of the `total` window area (`--multiplier-inverse-window-area`, which makes the single-loss weight 1 by definition). The API keywords are `weight_length_kind`, `extra_inverse_length_per_A`, `weight_length_seed_A`, `multiplier` and `multiplier_A`. Only the default choice, or an external multiplier equal to L_w, keeps the M4a model ID, values and comparison label; the other combinations report `simonsen1997_single_loss_spectrum_conventions_v0`, are always `not_matched` and add warnings. A weight length that differs from the multiplier lies outside the derivation of Eq. (19). The cutoff sensitivities hold L_w and m fixed. When the multiplier has an interval, the returned errors include it, so they can exceed the budget by up to about a factor of two; the effect of the weight-length uncertainty through the kernel is not included. [Design (Japanese)](docs/M4A2_DESIGN.md) · [Pre-registration (Japanese)](docs/M4A2_PREREG.md)
- **Comparison label.** In `run.json`, `comparison` reports `matched` only when the numerical conditions of the run equal one of the registered conditions in the distributed table of completed comparisons (Al and Si from the ELF data set with the registered primaries, output edges, windows, lengths and tolerances) and every required key of that record passed. `kernel_conditions_checked` means that only the kernel conditions agree; anything else is `not_matched`. The synthetic example above lies outside the registered conditions, so it is not `matched` and reports a warning. `physical_validity` is always `not_established`.
- **Outside the scope.** Multiple scattering, oblique emission, trajectory-dependent or self-consistent lengths, elastic scattering, losses outside the window, separation of intrinsic losses, instrument broadening, background subtraction and fitting are not implemented. For a material with active oscillators and a gap, windows that straddle the gap edge or lie below it are rejected. This is not a complete implementation of QUEELS-XPS.

[Equations and units (Japanese)](docs/EQUATIONS.md) · [Design (Japanese)](docs/M4A_DESIGN.md) · [Pre-registration (Japanese)](docs/M4A_PREREG.md)

Numerical non-convergence in the API raises `NumericalConvergenceError` or one of its subclasses. The CLI reports it as `not_converged` and invalid input as `error`. Both exit with code 2 before a new output directory is created. `dielectric` and `xps-fixed-depth` include `warning_count` in their success JSON. Their `run.json` diagnostics use `static_sign` and `kinematics`; the changelog lists the earlier names. JSON on stdout and stderr escapes non-ASCII characters, so it stays valid whatever the encoding of the console, pipe or file; `run.json` is UTF-8 and keeps them as written. [Changelog](CHANGELOG.md)

## Verification

Run `python tests/run_suite.py` from the source tree. The runner always tests the `src/` of its own tree: it puts `src/` first on the module search path and in `PYTHONPATH` for child processes, prints the path it tests, stops if the imported xpsloss is not the one in `src/`, and fails if no test runs. `python -m unittest discover -s tests -v` also works with `PYTHONPATH=src` or an editable install (`pip install -e .`); otherwise `tests/test_suite_source.py` fails, because the suite would be testing an installed copy. Running a single test file with `unittest` skips this check. The suite checks analytical limits, sum rules, convergence, fixed-depth terms against an independent numerical integration, and the comparison script using synthetic files. Agents independent of the implementer reviewed the equations and the implementation. There has been no human peer review. A separate reviewer agent attempted to reproduce Figs. 3, 5, and 8 with independent code using the dielectric function in Eq. (20) of Simonsen et al. (1997), outside this package's oscillator model. Of 84 comparison points, 36 lie within the figure-reading uncertainty, 18 lie outside it, and 30 depend on the assumed width (a uniform rule based on Table I of the paper; computed peaks refined beyond the grid). ### Comparison with QUEELS-XPS v2.1

For dispersive Al from the ELF data set at 1000 eV, normal emission, and a depth of 9.967 Å, both momentum choices failed the prespecified pointwise and integrated-area criteria over 1.0–99.8 eV. The maximum absolute difference in `total`, relative to the maximum reference `total` over that interval, was 6.6% for `k_total` and 7.5% for `k_parallel`. [Original criteria and results (Japanese)](docs/M2_FIRST_CASE.md)

The follow-up hypothesis and decision rule were recorded in local Git before three new synthetic runs. Given the convergence of the reference components at low loss, the follow-up used 5.0–99.8 eV and the depth nearest 10 Å in each run. With `k_total` and a candidate expression using the undispersed oscillator energy in the numerator of the real part of the inverse dielectric function, the maximum absolute difference for each of the three components was within 0.3% of the maximum reference `total` for all three inputs. With the published expression, at least one component exceeded this threshold for each input. [Preregistered follow-up (Japanese)](docs/QUEELS_DISPERSION_TEST.md)

xpsloss uses the published Eq. (32) of Yubero et al. (1996). The comparison tested v2.1 numerical output under the stated conditions. Its internal implementation, the cause of the residual difference of order 10⁻³, and accuracy against measured spectra remain unverified. Reference-software output is excluded from distribution.
