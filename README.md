# xpsloss

xpsloss is a Python library and command-line tool for researchers reproducing published models of electron energy loss in X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS). Version 0.1.0 covers oscillator energy-loss functions (ELFs), bulk differential inverse inelastic mean free paths in an infinite medium, causal dielectric response including hard gaps, and fixed-depth XPS cross sections. [日本語版](README.ja.md)

For emission at 0° from the surface normal and a positive creation depth, xpsloss implements a fixed-depth effective cross section based on Eqs. (13)–(17) of Simonsen, Yubero and Tougaard (1997), for gapless materials and, in the development version, for materials with a hard band gap (below). The caller chooses the momentum argument of the dielectric function. `k_parallel` uses the approximation made in the closed-form derivation. At normal emission, `k_total` recovers the dispersive bulk limit. The two choices are checked differently. An independent numerical route (I3) covers `k_parallel` for dispersionless and dispersive inputs. `k_total` is checked through the bulk identity of term 13a, the deep limit, and a second implementation of the same closed forms; for dispersive inputs, no independent check covers all finite-depth terms. For dispersionless inputs the two choices coincide. Following the paper, the model assumes that the electron and the core hole are created suddenly at the same point at t = 0, that the hole is static with infinite lifetime, that the electron moves on a rectilinear trajectory at a constant velocity set by the initial kinetic energy (nonrelativistic), that losses are small compared with E0, and a specular-reflection model of the surface. Oblique emission, depth averaging, and full spectra are outside this release; the development version adds depth averaging with an external constant length (below). [Equations and units (Japanese)](docs/EQUATIONS.md)

xpsloss is independent of QUEELS-XPS, QUASES and their authors. It is not an official, endorsed, or compatibility-guaranteed version of QUEELS-XPS and uses none of its executables, DLLs, source code, or figures.

## Quick start

From the root of the v0.1.0 source tree, use Python 3.10 or later in a virtual environment. The example material is synthetic.

```sh
python -m pip install .
xpsloss capabilities --json
xpsloss validate examples/synthetic_bulk.json
xpsloss bulk examples/synthetic_bulk.json --out runs/bulk
xpsloss dielectric examples/synthetic_bulk.json --q-inv-A 0.5 --out runs/dielectric
xpsloss xps-fixed-depth examples/synthetic_bulk.json --depth-A 10 --epsilon-momentum k_total --out runs/fixed-depth
xpsloss xps-depth-averaged examples/synthetic_bulk.json --weight-length-A 20 --epsilon-momentum k_total --out runs/depth-averaged  # development version
```

Give each calculation a new output directory. The CSV contains values on the loss-energy grid. For fixed depth these include the total, the electron contribution with zero hole charge, and the signed unit-hole contribution including interference, in Å⁻¹ eV⁻¹ per path length in the solid. The accompanying `run.json` records the input path as supplied, its SHA-256, units, model, numerical settings, and runtime versions.

For `xps-fixed-depth`, `run.json` also has an `applicability` block. It records the model assumptions, the largest loss divided by E0, the checked bounds (E0 of 1000–4000 eV, max T/E0 of 0.1) with their basis, and whether the run lies inside them. At both energy bounds, all eight terms were checked against the independent route with `k_parallel` at discrete losses up to T/E0 = 0.1. Depth, material, line widths, dispersion, hole charge and the lower end of the loss grid are not part of this check. A successful run outside either bound reports a warning. Being inside the bounds establishes neither accuracy for arbitrary inputs nor physical validity.

For a material with a hard band gap (development version, M2-2), the dielectric response is the step-function ELF with a Kramers–Kronig real part and no extra broadening. Losses within a narrow exclusion width of the gap edge are rejected. Below the gap the model medium is lossless: a loss is computed only when an outward-rounded analytic bound proves that no undamped surface mode (ε = −1) lies in its momentum range, and is otherwise rejected with `FixedDepthGapDomainError`. At such losses the unit-hole `total` vanishes, while `no_hole` and `hole` are nonzero reactive terms, not absorption. Gapped inputs must also satisfy a per-oscillator monotonicity condition. The gapped checks cover Si from the ELF data set at 1000 eV, depths of 2–200 Å and losses of 0.2–99.8 eV; other gapped inputs get a warning. [Design (Japanese)](docs/M2_2_DESIGN.md)

In the development version (M3a), `effective_cross_section_depth_averaged` and the `xps-depth-averaged` command average the fixed-depth outputs over depth at normal emission with the single-scattering path weight of Eq. (18) of Simonsen et al. (1997), x exp(−x/L)/L². The weight length L (`weight_length_A`, `--weight-length-A`) is required, supplied by the caller and constant over all paths; `--weight-length-source` records where it came from. The depth integral is closed analytically inside the momentum integrand, so there is no depth grid. The eleven outputs are in Å⁻¹ eV⁻¹ per path length in the solid, including vacuum losses, and `total = no_hole + hole_charge * hole`. As the paper cautions, Eq. (18) borrows a REELS path weighting whose validity for XPS needs examination, and the result describes only the first inelastic scattering, including intrinsic losses; both cautions are in `warnings`. Trajectory-dependent or self-consistent lengths, elastic scattering, non-uniform depth distributions, loss-window areas and spectra are not implemented. Non-convergence raises `DepthAverageConvergenceError` with a `reason`. The pre-registered checks used Al and Si from the ELF data set and synthetic inputs, almost all at 1000 eV, with lengths of 5, 20 and 80 Å and limits from 10⁻⁶ to 10⁶ Å, for both momentum choices. `run.json` lists the checked conditions but does not judge each run against them. [Design (Japanese)](docs/M3A_DESIGN.md) · [Pre-registration (Japanese)](docs/M3A_PREREG.md)

Numerical non-convergence in the API raises `NumericalConvergenceError` or one of its subclasses. The CLI reports it as `not_converged` and invalid input as `error`. Both exit with code 2 before a new output directory is created. `dielectric` and `xps-fixed-depth` include `warning_count` in their success JSON. Their `run.json` diagnostics use `static_sign` and `kinematics`; the changelog lists the earlier names. JSON on stdout and stderr escapes non-ASCII characters, so it stays valid whatever the encoding of the console, pipe or file; `run.json` is UTF-8 and keeps them as written. [Changelog](CHANGELOG.md)

## Verification

Run `python tests/run_suite.py` (or `python -m unittest discover -s tests -v`) from the source tree; the runner also fails if no test runs. The suite checks analytical limits, sum rules, convergence, fixed-depth terms against an independent numerical integration, and the comparison script using synthetic files. Agents independent of the implementer reviewed the equations and the implementation. There has been no human peer review. A separate reviewer agent attempted to reproduce Figs. 3, 5, and 8 with independent code using the dielectric function in Eq. (20) of Simonsen et al. (1997), outside this package's oscillator model. Of 84 comparison points, 36 lie within the figure-reading uncertainty, 18 lie outside it, and 30 depend on the assumed width (a uniform rule based on Table I of the paper; computed peaks refined beyond the grid). [Per-point results](benchmarks/paper_figures/RESULTS.md) [Validation record (Japanese)](docs/VALIDATION.md)

With M3a, the 275 tests of the development version (0.2.0.dev0) pass from the source tree on Windows with Python 3.12.10 (13 skipped: the full-record checks, which run only on request, and the check that was not run, below). For M3a, a reference author separate from the implementer computed 498 reference keys in multiple precision without the package code, and the implementation agrees with them within the pre-registered tolerances; all 2041 pre-registered runs end in their registered states (success, rejection or non-convergence). A numerical depth quadrature of the public fixed-depth function agrees at the 630 of 656 points where it has a value; the other 26 points have no value, so that check is incomplete. The planned comparison with the independent route I3 for `k_parallel` was not run because of its cost. All 27 deliberate faults that an independent reviewer injected were detected. ### Comparison with QUEELS-XPS v2.1

For dispersive Al from the ELF data set at 1000 eV, normal emission, and a depth of 9.967 Å, both momentum choices failed the prespecified pointwise and integrated-area criteria over 1.0–99.8 eV. The maximum absolute difference in `total`, relative to the maximum reference `total` over that interval, was 6.6% for `k_total` and 7.5% for `k_parallel`. [Original criteria and results (Japanese)](docs/M2_FIRST_CASE.md)

The follow-up hypothesis and decision rule were recorded in local Git before three new synthetic runs. Given the convergence of the reference components at low loss, the follow-up used 5.0–99.8 eV and the depth nearest 10 Å in each run. With `k_total` and a candidate expression using the undispersed oscillator energy in the numerator of the real part of the inverse dielectric function, the maximum absolute difference for each of the three components was within 0.3% of the maximum reference `total` for all three inputs. With the published expression, at least one component exceeded this threshold for each input. [Preregistered follow-up (Japanese)](docs/QUEELS_DISPERSION_TEST.md)

xpsloss uses the published Eq. (32) of Yubero et al. (1996). The comparison tested v2.1 numerical output under the stated conditions. Its internal implementation, the cause of the residual difference of order 10⁻³, and accuracy against measured spectra remain unverified. Reference-software output is excluded from distribution.
